# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

Welcome to the OctoAcme project management documentation. These documents describe how cross-functional projects are initiated, planned, delivered, released, and improved.

## Project management processes at a glance

OctoAcme uses a structured project lifecycle: initiation, planning, execution, release, and retrospective. Initiation validates the business need, defines measurable outcomes, identifies stakeholders, confirms the initial timeline and risks, and decides whether work should proceed. Approved initiatives move into planning, where teams turn the project into an actionable backlog by defining scope, estimates, acceptance criteria, a Definition of Done, milestones, dependencies, and risks.

During execution, delivery is coordinated through a project board with Backlog, Ready, In Progress, In Review, QA, and Done stages. The team follows small-PR conventions, automated CI testing and linting, required review approval, regular demos, and an escalation path for blockers. The Project Manager coordinates schedules, risks, communications, and delivery; the Product Manager owns outcomes, prioritizes the backlog, and measures success; Developers implement, test, and document the solution; and QA validates quality and acceptance. Stakeholders provide input and approvals throughout.

Communication maintains shared visibility through daily standups, twice-weekly delivery team meetings where agreed, weekly PM–Product alignment, milestone demonstrations, weekly status updates, and monthly stakeholder reports. Risks, dependencies, and blockers are tracked in a single source of truth and escalated from the team to the PM, Product Lead, dependent teams, or sponsor according to impact.

Quality assurance is integrated throughout delivery. Unit and integration tests cover new logic, critical flows receive end-to-end smoke tests, CI includes linting and security scans, and manual QA may validate feature acceptance. Before release, teams confirm that acceptance criteria and PR reviews are complete, CI and security checks pass, release notes and rollback plans are ready, and smoke tests succeed. After sprints, releases, milestones, or incidents, retrospectives capture lessons and convert priority improvements into owned, dated backlog actions for continuous improvement.
